CBSE Class 10 trigonometry is mostly **identity algebra**. Three forms of one relationship do 80% of the work.

## Identity 1 — Pythagorean

<Math math="\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1" display />

**Use when:** Expressing sin in terms of cos (or vice versa), simplifying √(1 − sin²θ).

## Identity 2 — Tangent form

Divide identity 1 by **cos²θ** (cos θ ≠ 0):

<Math math="1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta" display />

**Use when:** Questions mix **tan** and **sec**, or you see 1 + tan² on one side.

## Identity 3 — Cotangent form

Divide identity 1 by **sin²θ** (sin θ ≠ 0):

<Math math="1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta" display />

**Use when:** Cot and cosec appear together — common in NCERT Exercise 8.4.

<Callout title="Boards marking">
Write the identity you start from. One line of substitution often earns a step mark even if the final simplification has a slip.
</Callout>

## Worked example (NCERT style)

**Prove:** (sec θ − tan θ)(sec θ + tan θ) = 1

Left side = sec²θ − tan²θ = **1** (identity 2 rearranged).

<Math math="\sec^2\theta - \tan^2\theta = 1" display />

## Values table — don’t over-memorise

Know **0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°** for sin and cos; tan = sin/cos. For 15° and 75°, derive once using:

<Math math="\sin 15° = \sin(45° - 30°)" display />
