In IBPS and SSC prelims you rarely get “hard” arithmetic — you get **fast** arithmetic. Multiplying by 11 is one of the highest-frequency operations once you start chaining simplifications.

## The rule

For a two-digit number **ab** (tens digit `a`, units digit `b`):

1. Write the **first digit** `a` on the left.
2. Write **a + b** in the middle.
3. Write the **last digit** `b` on the right.
4. If the middle sum is **10 or more**, carry **1** to the left digit.

<Math math="34 \times 11 = 374" display />

Walkthrough for 34:

- Left: **3**
- Middle: **3 + 4 = 7**
- Right: **4** → **374**

## When the middle sum carries

Take **57 × 11**:

- Left starts as **5**, middle **5 + 7 = 12** → write **2**, carry **1** → left becomes **6**
- Right: **7**
- Answer: **627**

<Math math="57 \times 11 = 627" display />

<Callout title="Exam tip">
Practice ten random two-digit numbers aloud before Sunday’s live mock. Muscle memory beats re-deriving the rule under pressure.
</Callout>

## Three-digit numbers (bonus)

For **abc × 11**, work right to left with the same “add neighbours” idea:

<Math math="123 \times 11 = 1353" display />

- Units: **3**
- Tens: **2 + 3 = 5**
- Hundreds: **1 + 2 = 3**
- Thousands: **1**

## Where this shows up

- **Percentage chains** after you rewrite a fraction as ×11/100.
- **Successive discounts** when one factor is 1.1.
- **Time–speed** back-calculations when speed is given as “11 km/h faster than…”

## Drill

Solve in under 8 seconds each: **26×11**, **48×11**, **91×11**, **73×11**.

Check: 286, 528, 1001, 803.
